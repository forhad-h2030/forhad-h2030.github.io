AI/ML · Spin Physics · Simulations

I am a Postdoctoral Researcher at the University of Virginia, advised by Dr. Dustin Keller. I obtained my Ph.D. in Physics from New Mexico State University, advised by Dr. Stephen Pate.

I am especially interested in machine learning and AI-based simulation optimization, and in extracting signals in regimes where traditional methods become more challenging, including tuning NMR signals in the polarized target environment.

I study polarized-target instrumentation and detector optimization for experimental nuclear particle physics, which I have been doing throughout my graduate and postdoctoral research. My research has focused on AFP spin-reversal and target operations for the SpinQuest (E1039) experiment at Fermilab, alongside ML frameworks (normalizing flows, DNNs) for combinatoric background modeling, including experimental extraction of the transverse single-spin asymmetry (TSSA) in Drell–Yan and J/ψ production from the SpinQuest and SeaQuest (E906) experiments.

Iterative ML & Monte Carlo tuning

An Iterative Machine Learning Framework for Event Classification and Monte Carlo Tuning in SpinQuest

An iterative loop between ML classification and Monte Carlo tuning: classifier-tagged combinatoric events guide the tuning of simulated dimuon-mass and kinematic distributions to match data, which in turn sharpens the next round of classification.
